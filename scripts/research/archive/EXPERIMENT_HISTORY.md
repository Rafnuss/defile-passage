# Hourly weather pilot

The active development now follows [SIMPLE_RATE_PLAN.md](../SIMPLE_RATE_PLAN.md): a small count-rate GAM with direct aggregation of hourly expected counts to intact survey totals. The user requires simplicity and speed. The hurdle/Gamma/convolution experiments below are historical diagnostics, not the architecture being extended. The five scientific assessment goals remain in [GAP_FILL_PLAN.md](../GAP_FILL_PLAN.md).

The active work sequence and corrected assessment are tracked in [GAP_FILL_PLAN.md](../GAP_FILL_PLAN.md). The original five-step order is assessment/numerics → positive-count calibration → shared daily state → uncertainty/year validation → mixed temporal scales/history. Earlier daily wet/dry score paragraphs are historical and are superseded by the common-weather-classification review described there.

This experiment splits complete observed Trektellen surveys independently of bird entries, fits raw hourly counts with real-duration exposure, and tests whether weather improves prediction of contiguous withheld intervals. It reads the current defile-dataset release and makes a bounded snapshot of the local ERA5 cache. It imports no forecast code and does not change Explore's production models or exports.

The builder defaults to completed seasons 2022–2025 and four pilot taxa: Red Kite, Common Buzzard, European Honey Buzzard and Common Wood Pigeon. Override `--years`, `--taxa` and `--out` to extend the pilot. R requires mgcv, dplyr, tidyr, readr, ggplot2, here, cli and MASS; run the script section by section to inspect its intermediate objects. Optional R arguments override the input and output folders, in that order.

## Observation accounting

- `hourly_effort.csv` contains each complete observed survey cut at UTC clock-hour boundaries, including empty and partial hours. Calendar-day weather closures are not observed effort.
- `hourly_counts.csv` contains main-direction counts with supported hourly placement, for all taxa in the selected seasons. Exact timestamps are assigned to their hour. Own or inherited intervals are assigned only when wholly contained within one clock hour and their survey.
- `unresolved_counts.csv` retains daily/untimed counts, intervals crossing hour boundaries and timestamps outside the released survey. Entries are never moved, redistributed or silently discarded.
- `model_frame.csv` joins effort to the selected species, including species-specific zeros. An unresolved positive or presence-only record makes all hours of that species' survey unsuitable for hourly fitting. Their `count` is missing, while `timed_count` retains the supported lower bound. An untimed explicit zero does not invalidate the survey.
- `weather_stops.csv` keeps documented weather closures separately. Their zeros are assumptions and are not training or validation observations.
- `timing_reconciliation.csv`, `species_summary.csv` and `metadata.json` record conservation of birds and effort, exclusions, units, source paths and SHA-256 hashes.

Survey completeness is used under the dataset's assumption of systematic species recording. Exact survey edges are retained. The first fit uses only intervals wholly within civil dawn/dusk (sun altitude −6°); boundary and night intervals remain in the prepared data and their exclusions are reported in `eligibility.csv`. It does not interpret missing dates or unobserved hours as zero.

## Weather and time

The predictor `tau` is the fraction of that local day's civil daylight elapsed at the observation interval's midpoint. Duration remains in real hours. The GAM is an hourly approximation: it evaluates the rate at the interval midpoint rather than integrating its varying diurnal curve within the hour. The time-coordinate transformation does not normalize predicted counts or force equal daily totals.

The weather snapshot uses the forecast cache's existing converted units. Precipitation in metres is converted to millimetres; accumulated solar radiation in J/m² is converted to W/m². [Open-Meteo defines rain and radiation at timestamp t over the preceding hour](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api), so the builder aligns the value at t+1 hour with count interval \[t, t+1 hour). The previous-24-hour rain summary excludes the current interval. Wind components and low cloud are approximated by the mean of the instantaneous values at the two endpoints. Lags are computed on a complete hourly weather index before selecting observation times, retaining missingness across any cache gaps.

## First model and comparison

Both models are negative-binomial GAMs with `offset(log(exposure_hours))`, separate year levels, a seasonal curve, a noncyclic daylight-time curve, a season × daylight-time interaction and an independent random effect for each day. The weather model adds current precipitation, preceding-24-hour precipitation, wind u/v, solar radiation and low cloud. Precipitation predictors use log1p. Smoothing and dispersion are estimated separately within each training fold using mgcv::bam. Neither model includes the current episode smooth. With four recent seasons, this pilot estimates year levels rather than a long-term trend or gradual phenology shift.

Three folds withhold seven-day calendar blocks, balanced across years, and test each eligible interval once. A fourth fold hides up to three adjacent intervals around the wettest observed hour, or the central hour on dry days, retaining other hours on the same day. Masks depend on dates, weather and effort, never bird counts. A partly observed day is conditioned on its remaining hours through its random effect. Wholly withheld days receive draws from the learned distribution of day effects, rather than their full-data fitted effects. Daily totals sum the same posterior predictive draws, preserving shared-day and coefficient uncertainty. Intervals condition on estimated smoothing and dispersion parameters; they do not account for weather measurement error.

`logs/hourly-weather/` contains fitted models, hourly and daily held-out predictions, wet/dry scores, dispersion and residual diagnostics, partial precipitation-effect curves, figures and the R session information. Scores concern the observed intervals that were hidden, not an independently known dawn-to-dusk count. The rain effect is conditional on the other predictors and is not a causal estimate. Diagnostics include basis checks and concurvity because radiation, rain and cloud may overlap strongly.

This is a pilot for deciding whether to develop the interval-integrated model. It does not yet fit historical daily totals, fill production gaps, estimate residual correlation across days, establish zero passage in unobserved severe weather, or provide a count distribution invariant to arbitrary temporal aggregation. Negative-binomial overdispersion is per observation; posterior predictive daily sums share day effects but retain conditional hourly independence. Review blocked-validation bias and interval coverage before interpreting any model as an improvement.

Each pilot species has approximately 1,500 observed wet intervals. ## Issue-fixing experiment

The revised run writes to `logs/hourly-weather-v2/`, preserving the first run. `validation_masks.csv` supplies the identical held-out intervals to R and the production comparison. Week masks use non-leap seasonal dates. Short masks stay within uninterrupted effort: two neighbouring rows separated by an observation break cannot belong to the same hidden gap. Masks use weather, time and effort only. The builder also retains a complete civil-daylight weather grid over each year's observed calendar range, including dates without surveys and documented closures; these are prediction contexts, never added zeros.

`hourly_uncertainty.R` adapts the production engine's `trend.MIN_RATE` information convention to real-hour exposure. For a training interval of duration E in a civil day of length L hours, the minimum mean used for approximate information is 0.1 × E / L. Extra negative-binomial information is added to the inverse coefficient covariance, leaving fitted coefficients, smoothing parameters, dispersion and all observations unchanged. This regularizes the Gaussian approximation around near-zero fitted rates; it is not additional observed birds, a constraint on passage, or an exact non-Gaussian posterior. The pilot applies it after fitting, whereas the production engine also uses its information convention in hyperparameter estimation. Sensitivity is evaluated at 0.01, 0.1 and 1 bird per daylight day using training information only, without selecting a floor using hidden counts. Raw mgcv summaries retain their original covariance; predictive simulations and rain-effect intervals use the adjusted covariance.

Predicted means use the analytic log-normal expectation, exp(estimated log mean + log-variance / 2), rather than averaging a small set of exponentiated coefficient draws. New days include the fitted daily-effect variance. This removes Monte Carlo instability in means, but does not cure misspecification of the daily-effect distribution. Five hundred shared coefficient/day draws still produce count intervals and hourly predictive log scores. `floor_sensitivity.csv` reports hourly errors and aggregate count bias; it does not report daily MAE. A synthetic zero-tail regression checks that the covariance correction reduces extreme variance without changing the observations or exposure scaling.

Four hourly variants are evaluated: `baseline`, `weather`, `weather_time` (season k = 30, daylight k = 16, interaction k = 10 × 12), and `weather_reduced` (original time bases and weather without radiation/low cloud). These separate time flexibility from weather simplification. They are exploratory candidates, not automatically selected replacements. `basis_checks.csv`, `concurvity.csv` and their review tables retain diagnostics; low basis-test p-values alone do not select a more complex model, especially with flocking and shared-day effects.

`compare_production.py` calls the actual `trend.fit`, `trend.fill_draws` and flock-dispersion implementation, refitting all hyperparameters inside each fold. It aggregates the same eligible 2022–2025 intervals to daily counts and learns the production ratio-based profile from retained intervals, using their exact daylight midpoints. Profile normalization and five-minute solar-grid coverage follow production conventions; hourly counts for flock dispersion are assigned by solar midpoint before four-hour grouping. This is a comparison of the production architecture on matched pilot inputs, not of previously exported fits or the production model trained on all historical data. Hidden counts reconcile exactly across models. `production_comparison.csv` contains daily errors, bias and interval coverage; `paired_mae_differences.csv` resamples calendar-week clusters for uncertainty in paired MAE differences. Candidate exploration on these masks is not independent confirmation.

`check_weather_support.py` measures observed and missing real-hour exposure in rain bands and marks extrapolation beyond observed feature ranges. It also flags sparse weather combinations using standardized nearest-neighbour distance relative to the 95th percentile of observed leave-one-out distances. That is a support diagnostic, not a calibrated reliability probability, and it does not assess seasonal support or weather measurement error. Missing exposure includes all unobserved or ineligible daylight intervals; it is not attributed entirely to weather. Closure exposure is restricted to the released closure bounds intersected with daylight. `closure_sensitivity.R` compares weather predictions for these intervals against the explicit zero-passage assumption, retaining their counts as unknown and reporting unsupported exposure. Neither assumption is validated by the absence of counting.

## Daily multiplier experiment (10 October 2026)

The next experiment replaces the Gaussian day effect with a population rate and a mean-one Gamma daily multiplier, fitted by integrating the multiplier out of the training likelihood. The data, four species, year effects, weather predictors, daylight-time bases and held-out masks are unchanged. This is a new fitted model, not a rescaling of the Gaussian model's predictions.

```bash
Rscript scripts/research/prepare_daily_rate.R > logs/hourly-weather-v3-prepare.log 2>&1
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS=1 uv run python scripts/research/fit_daily_rate.py > logs/hourly-weather-v3-fit.log 2>&1
Rscript scripts/research/review_daily_rate.R > logs/hourly-weather-v3-review.log 2>&1
```

`prepare_daily_rate.R` fits training-only independent negative-binomial weather GAMs, exports their design matrices and smoothing penalties, and saves their fits. `fit_daily_rate.py` uses those same bases and holds the smoothing penalties fixed while jointly estimating population coefficients and daily/count shapes by penalized marginal likelihood. Its default refits every model; `--reuse-fits` is only for resuming this unchanged prepared experiment. `day_rate.py` contains the reusable likelihood, quadrature and observed-information calculations. Twenty training contexts (four masks and a final fit per species) yield 60 model fits/reference contexts. All outputs are under `logs/hourly-weather-v3/`; production exports are unchanged.

For interval i on day d, the population expected count is mu_i = E_i × exp(X_i beta), where E is real-hour exposure and X contains the existing seasonal, normalized civil-daylight-time and weather terms. The latent multiplier g_d follows Gamma(shape a, rate a), independently across days: E(g_d) = 1 and Var(g_d) = 1/a. `gamma_day_poisson` uses Y_i conditional on g_d ~ Poisson(g_d mu_i). `gamma_day_flocks` instead uses NB(mean g_d mu_i, shape kappa × E_i), allowing additional within-day flock variability. `weather_independent` is the training-only NB GAM without a daily effect, with mgcv's original constant per-interval count shape, and isolates the effect of removing daily dependence.

The Gamma-Poisson marginal likelihood and partially observed day's multiplier posterior are analytic. The Gamma-NB likelihood uses adaptive split Gauss-Legendre integration in log multiplier, including the change-of-variable Jacobian. Integration boundaries are 32 log-density units below the mode; 61 nodes on each side are checked against 121 on fitted training likelihoods. The maximum difference is below 4e-11 log-likelihood units. Unit checks compare independent numerical integration, analytic gradients and observed information, verify the prior mean/variance, and verify count aggregation at constant rate. A shape proportional to exposure makes NB aggregation consistent within a constant-rate interval, conditional on the common daily multiplier. When rate or weather changes, expected counts must still be integrated piecewise; this does not implement arbitrary-duration historical likelihoods or exact within-hour integration.

Coefficient covariance comes from the observed marginal beta Hessian with fitted shapes and smoothing penalties held fixed. The Gamma-NB calculation subtracts the posterior variance of the complete-data score (Louis' identity), retaining dependence across a day's hours. The same 0.1-bird/day exposure-scaled information floor regularizes empty tails without changing fitted rates or observations. For fully hidden days, the predictive mean is E × exp(X beta + X V X'/2): the daily multiplier contributes no extra log-normal inflation. For short gaps, only retained observations condition that day's multiplier, conditional on each coefficient draw. Five hundred shared draws provide count intervals and hourly log scores; short-gap means average the conditional expected count and have their Monte Carlo standard error recorded in `held_out_hours.csv`. These are approximate empirical-Bayes predictions, without shape/smoothing uncertainty. Smoothing selection is anchored to an independent NB fit rather than jointly optimized under the integrated daily likelihood, an explicit limitation of this comparison.

The Gamma-Poisson candidate is rejected: excluding hourly flock variability produces extreme Pigeon/Honey Buzzard means. Gamma + flocks removes the automatic log-normal day-mean inflation and improves the largest Pigeon whole-day failures, but still overpredicts dry days and short rainy gaps. Before using it for missing totals, jointly fitting the smoothing penalties with the integrated daily/count distribution and checking residual temporal and within-day weather structure remain priorities. The production model still performs better on dry whole-day gaps for Buzzard, Red Kite and Pigeon. Exact interval integration, mixed-duration historical data and full-history comparisons remain open.

## Species coverage and smoothing follow-up (11 October 2026)

The four-species pilot was too narrow to select a model. `select_weather_species.py` audits recent counts, complete-survey coverage, positive days, rainy positive days, within-hour zeros and concentration of counts, then writes the documented panel in `weather_species.tsv`. The 12 priority targets are Common Buzzard, Red Kite, European Honey Buzzard, Common Wood Pigeon, Black Kite, Eurasian Sparrowhawk, Common Kestrel, Western Marsh Harrier, Stock Dove, Grey Heron, Great Cormorant and White Stork. Four passerines—Eurasian Chaffinch, Barn Swallow, Meadow Pipit and Redwing—are a separate comparison group. The user confirmed that all passerines were counted throughout complete surveys in 2022–2025; the model treats an absent entry as zero only within that period and only for a complete survey. This confirmation does not extend to earlier years. The priority-target scope uses the 2023–2025 report descriptions and assumes continuity for 2022 because the 2022 annual report is unavailable. See `../defile-dataset/docs/sampling-history.md` and the input metadata for that limitation. Coverage gates training and validation rows before zeros are constructed, so unknown recording history is missing rather than a biological zero.

`prepare_daily_rate.R` and `fit_daily_rate.py` were rerun for both groups on the same 2022–2025 survey and weather framework. The final panel comprises 80 species/fold contexts (five contexts per species), including folds for seven-day gaps, within-day gaps and final fits. Each context compares the production episode model refitted on the same training data, an independent weather NB GAM, an integrated mean-one Gamma day effect with hourly NB flock variation and a version that also updates GAM smoothing penalties. The latter uses an approximate generalized Fellner–Schall/posterior-moment direction with refitting and evidence-based backtracking; it is an exploratory empirical-Bayes procedure, not an exact global optimizer. Outputs and the reproducible combined check are under `logs/hourly-weather-panel-v4/`; run `Rscript scripts/research/review_species_panel.R` after fitting to verify coverage scope, matching truths, quadrature, hourly/daily reconciliation and score summaries.

For species-balanced whole-day MAE, the smoothed Gamma model averages 0.93 times the production error on wet hidden days (11/16 species lower) and 1.25 times production error on dry hidden days (3/16 lower). The paired week-block bootstrap averaged across species does not resolve a wet-day advantage over production (MAE difference −15 birds; mean species-level intervals span −76 to +38), while dry-day error is higher (mean difference +109; intervals +33 to +206). Within-day gaps are mixed: the smoothed model's mean species-balanced MAE is 1.13 times production on dry gaps and 1.00 times production on wet gaps. These comparisons use selected species and reused masks, so they are exploratory rather than independent confirmation. A rainy-day advantage cannot by itself establish correct totals for unobserved closures.

The expanded weather-support audit finds about 60–65 observed species-hours per taxon at ≥3 mm/hour across the four seasons. All 12 priority taxa have 89–100% zero-count hours in that band; passerines range from 77–96%, with 1,070 birds across 261 observed species-hours. This is consistent with a strong rain-related reduction in counts, but the pooled values are not adjusted for season or daylight and the high-rain sample remains small. The documented stop intervals amount to 58.3 unique daylight hours; about 27 hours are outside the observed weather-combination support. Keep these closure hours unknown: the available observations do not validate a hard-zero rule.

Updating the smoothing penalties barely changes the result relative to the fixed-penalty Gamma + flocks fit: the mean species-level daily MAE change is about zero on wet gaps and −5 birds on dry gaps, with paired intervals spanning zero. Of 80 smoothing searches, 32 ended with a stalled direction, 16 reached the 40-step limit, 30 stopped at an evidence plateau and only two met the moment-direction tolerance. Every accepted step decreased the negative restricted-evidence score, but these termination counts mean the procedure has not established stable smoothing hyperparameters across the panel. Retain fixed training-only smoothing as the interpretable comparison for now; do not select the iterative smoother as a model improvement.

This larger panel weakens the case for choosing a universal replacement: wet whole-day performance is promising but uncertain, while dry whole-day performance and species-level variation remain problematic. Keep the current production gap fill unchanged. Next, improve the day-level distribution and its calibration, investigate dry-day rate bias and validation robustness across years, and obtain protocol evidence for priority taxa in 2022 before extending the zero scope. Exact within-hour integration, joint smoothing selection under the integrated likelihood, historical mixed-duration data and a count distribution calibrated at arbitrary aggregation scales remain future work.

## Weather activity-gate experiment (11 October 2026)

`fit_weather_activity.py` adds a hurdle alternative on the same 16-species panel and held-out masks. It models whether an interval contains any birds with a complementary-log-log occurrence hazard and real-hour exposure, then models positive counts with an exposure-scaled, zero-truncated negative binomial. The activity and positive-count components use the existing training-only weather-GAM design matrices and smoothing penalties, so this is a focused test of separating occurrence from flock size. `review_weather_activity.py` checks that it predicts the same held-out rows and truths as production, the independent weather NB, Gamma + flocks and the smoothing variant, and that hourly means reconcile with their daily sums.

After correcting common daily weather classification and tightening independent activity fits, the mean species MAE ratio versus production is 0.94 on dry whole-day gaps and 0.75 on wet whole-day gaps. The equal-species mean paired absolute-MAE difference is +21.7 birds on dry days (panel week-bootstrap 95% interval +0.8 to +48.5) and −52.9 on wet days (−109.1 to −13.0). Mean species error ratios and mean absolute differences are different estimands and can differ in direction because of species count scales. Within-day MAE ratios are 1.26 on dry gaps and 0.95 on wet gaps. Calendar weeks are resampled jointly across species, with the panel statistic computed inside each replicate; species interval endpoints are no longer averaged. These are exploratory comparisons on selected species and reused masks, without model-selection uncertainty.

The pooled occurrence probabilities track the fraction of positive intervals across rain bands, but the species-level positive-count assessment remains unresolved. Evaluating each observed interval once in the whole-day folds, ≥3 mm/hour has 1,176 species-hours, 70 positive hours and 1,390 birds; predicted totals are 2,829. Most excess comes from Barn Swallow and Pigeon, with only one positive Pigeon hour in that band. A tenfold positive-smooth penalty improves ordinary gap MAE slightly but worsens heavy-rain predictions to 3,340 birds and is rejected as a calibration fix. Whole-day and short-gap tasks are reported separately to expose their overlapping observations. See [GAP_FILL_PLAN.md](../GAP_FILL_PLAN.md) and `review_positive_activity.py` for step 2.

This first activity model treats observed intervals independently given weather and time. It has no shared daily state, does not condition a hidden hour on the rest of its survey day and produces no calibrated predictive intervals. The next experiment below implements the shared state and evaluates it on held-out years. Keep documented closures unknown and production gap filling unchanged until daily uncertainty and remaining calibration issues are resolved.

## Shared daily weather-activity experiment (11 October 2026)

`fit_shared_activity.py` fits a complementary-log-log weather-sensitive occurrence gate and a zero-truncated negative-binomial positive-count model, with both sharing one latent Gamma daily multiplier. For a partially hidden day, the posterior daily multiplier is conditioned on all retained hourly counts from that survey date; for a wholly hidden day, predictions integrate over its prior. This makes the hourly predictions add exactly to daily predictions and allows the same fit to score both hourly and daily gaps. The Gamma shape is fixed at the value from the matched training-only Gamma-day/flocks fit, while the gate coefficients, positive-count coefficients and positive-count dispersion are refitted jointly. It excludes the episode effect. The current implementation uses normalized Gamma quadrature from a Jacobi matrix at every shape, with exact formulas for all-zero days and retained-zero conditioning.

`prepare_daily_rate.R ... year_holdout` builds four additional folds, each holding out an entire year from 2022 through 2025. The year factor is removed for these fits because the held-out year has no estimable fixed effect. `review_shared_activity_years.py` checks held-out truth matching, hourly-to-daily conservation, species-balanced MAE, and occurrence/count calibration by year and hourly rain band.
