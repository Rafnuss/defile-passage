## What the effort audit establishes

Run `uv run python scripts/research/audit_gap_target.py`. Outputs are in `logs/gap-target-audit/`. The denominator is the existing fixed site comparison window, 18 July–18 November, 124 days per year. Counted effort includes complete non-weather-stop intervals only; released intervals do not overlap. Civil daylight is an astronomical reference for comparing effort, not a verified species passage distribution or a declaration that all uncovered minutes require filling. Documented weather stops and incomplete surveys remain separate; no bird zeros are invented. Historical inferred continuity remains an input assumption inherited from the release.

The recent annual fractions are 76.6%, 91.2%, 87.4% and 87.9%. Historical short days are a major part of the target, alongside missing dates. Weather-stop documentation is not equally complete across eras: zero documented historical stops does not mean zero rain losses. ## What the completed tests establish, and what they do not

All active small-model validation uses 2022–2025. The whole-day tests hide one-, three- or seven-day blocks inside otherwise densely sampled years. The short-gap tests hide three adjacent intervals inside long observed runs: of 2,508 core species-cases, 28 (1.1%) retain less than half the observed effort, 850 retain half to three quarters, and 1,630 retain more than three quarters. These fractions refer to the observed run, not civil daylight, so they are not directly comparable with the audit's daylight fractions. They nevertheless show that very low retained information is almost absent from the current short-gap test.

The current rate model estimates an offset for each of four observed years. Removing a few days while retaining most of the season gives much better information about that offset than sparse historical sampling. Historical observations have been inventoried, not fitted to the active small model; their 43,489 recorded species-survey totals have unverified species scope. Historical absent entries cannot automatically become zeros. The current priority weather input spans 2022–2025; historical weather has not been assembled into this research input. Existing four-year results are not evidence for historical reconstruction or a completely unseen-year offset.

Retained observations already inform the operational GAM fit. The additional retained-count multiplier is an optional empirical prediction layer, not part of the general mean model's objective. Its selection and uncertainty calibration currently reflect dense recent sampling. Keep it separate until the general model has passed a relevant coverage test. Species-specific seasonal/time/weather parameters are consistent with one general model; adding species/year/coverage-specific correction rules would be a different development step.

## Next experiment: historical coverage transfer

1. Freeze the current weather model and matched original benchmark. Make the ten core large-bird species the primary group; report pigeons and passerines separately and retain every species result. Stop comparing further adjustment functions.
2. Create pseudo-historical seasons from 2022–2025 using actual historical month/day attendance windows, preserving their missing mornings/evenings, interrupted days, multi-day gaps and annual effort density together. Use representative templates from 1993–2007, 2008–2016 and 2017–2021, chosen by effort alone before examining prediction errors. Apply each template to each recent year. Restrict each template to modern intervals with valid observed truth; report the resulting coverage, discarded dates and unscorable minutes rather than pretending the whole modern daylight day was observed. Keep all species on the same site masks. Initially assess 1993 onward; assess the exceptionally sparse pre-1993 era separately as an extrapolation stress test.
3. Separate information loss from recording resolution. Fit the same retained data once as eligible hourly observations and once as intact survey totals over retained connected counting intervals. Sum true retained counts to create those totals; never distribute an original untimed total into fabricated hourly observations. Rebuild scaling, smoothing and year effects using retained training observations only. Both benchmarks receive exactly the same observed information. This is a crossed coverage/resolution experiment, not another species calibration search.
4. Add a separately reported weather-driven mask experiment. Hide wet runs and dry attendance gaps using weather and effort only, with durations and within-day timing matched to documented closures. Historical templates transplanted to another year do not automatically preserve a rain-related missingness mechanism. Modern observed rain intervals provide only partial support for this experiment; unobserved true storm closures have no count truth and cannot be scored as assumed zeros.
5. Score missing-count error and each reconstructed species-season total, including signed/relative bias, dry/wet totals, and performance by era template, recent year, gap length and retained coverage. Show equal-species summaries and median species ratios alongside individual species, so neither a flocking species nor pooled bias cancellation decides the outcome. Bootstrap entire recent years/template seasons jointly across species; four modern years provide limited independent evidence. Report the amount of each reconstructed season supported by observed truth. A total over scored modern windows is not an independently verified full-daylight seasonal total.
6. Compare the unconditional weather prediction with the original benchmark first. Then evaluate only the existing retained-count update as a prespecified additional arm, with its tuning/calibration repeated under the same sparse/coarse training masks. Never tune on hidden counts. The update must not be required for wholly missing days, and interval predictions must be recalibrated for this setting. Do not select another function from previously explored outcomes.

The key comparison is performance as annual retained information decreases, including whether a year offset remains estimable. Do not confuse this with completely unseen-year forecasting: a year with no eligible counts has no data-estimated year offset. If sparse-year offsets or time/weather effects fail, discuss a small structural change (for example, stronger partial pooling of year offsets) before adding local corrections. Establish the historical weather and recording scope needed for the affected years in parallel; retrospective coarse-interval holdouts become the next real-data check once those inputs are verified.

This design tests coverage transfer while controlling the true modern counts. It cannot by itself validate changes in observer protocol, migration timing, weather response or historical record completeness. If dawn/evening extrapolation lies outside all modern observed support, identify it separately rather than claiming a validated fill. Seasonal uncertainty also remains distinct from marginal hourly/daily intervals: correlated gap errors cannot be summed as independent draws.

## Frozen implementation choices before fitting

The initial batch contains 640 contexts: 16 species × four target years × five masks × two target-year recording resolutions. Each context keeps the other three modern years hourly and thins only its target year. This tests sparse-year offsets with shared seasonal/time/weather effects informed by other years, not an entirely coarse or sparse four-year training set. The original benchmark learns its time-of-day profile from the other three years in both arms, preventing hidden within-survey hourly information from leaking into the coarse comparison. Its daily fitting objective already sums observed counts and coverage, so its point predictions are identical between the hourly and survey arms under this fixed outside-year profile.

Templates were chosen by nearest median annual civil-daylight effort within each era, with earliest year breaking ties: 2007 (1993–2007), 2015 (2008–2016), 2021 (2017–2021), and 1978 (pre-1993 stress test). No bird counts entered this selection. The site mask retains an indivisible modern count interval only when it is wholly contained within the historical local-clock attendance window on the matching month/day. The resulting inward rounding of boundaries is recorded through realized effort; no original count is split or allocated. Hourly and coarse arms have exactly identical retained counts and durations. Coarse target-year counts are sums over connected retained intervals, not full-day totals with guessed exposure.

The separate weather mask hides observed intervals at ≥0.1 mm/hour and dry logistical three-day runs at non-leap day-of-year phase 5–7 of a 21-day cycle. It tests weather-dependent selection but does not match an empirically estimated historical closure-duration distribution; that remains limited by uneven closure documentation. Masked observed intervals provide truth; actual unobserved closures are not inserted as zeros.

The retained-update calibration applies the same mask to other modern years in three calendar-week groups, recreating coarse observations in calibration dates for the coarse arm. All hidden outer target counts stay excluded. The existing pseudo-count grid and MAE criterion are unchanged. Its inner bases/scaling and smoothing use the outer retained data, so these are approximate cross-validation residual intervals rather than strict split calibration. Wholly missing days receive no same-day update. No joint seasonal intervals are inferred from the marginal daily intervals.

Original point means integrate 500 coefficient draws and analytically average its conditional Gamma/count states, avoiding additional count-draw Monte Carlo noise. This is the same posterior mean estimand as `fill_draws`, not a new benchmark architecture. All batch outputs retain context identity, failed numerical checks, target-year effort and scored-window truth. This implementation is a bounded transfer test; a single median-effort template per era does not represent that era's full diversity.
