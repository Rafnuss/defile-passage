# Controlled ERA5 weather-variable test

> Completed experiment record. Its results are preserved; earlier next-step proposals below are superseded by the [consolidated model decision](MODEL_SPECIFICATION.md). Further experimentation is paused.

## Protocol fixed before fitting

The user requested better use of existing ERA5 variables before obtaining additional weather inputs. Test two separate small extensions of the retained hourly mean-rate reference, without daily latent states, retained-count corrections, richer smooths or duration weights. Keep intact survey counts, exact covered-hour mean aggregation, bounded UTC clock terms, common season and annual offsets, unweighted power-1.5 working loss and the bounded BFGS solver. Each extension adds two coefficients, taking the historical model from 51 to 53. The coefficients remain species-specific.

- Rain history: add log(1 + precipitation summed over the preceding 24 complete hours), plus its interaction with a currently dry hour (\<0.1 mm). The antecedent window excludes current-hour rain and crosses calendar-day boundaries. The main effect allows antecedent rain to modify passage during wet hours; the sum of the two coefficients gives its effect during dry hours. This permits either persistent suppression or elevated passage after rain, without assuming a rebound. No lag-window or rain-threshold search is scheduled.
- Soaring conditions: add hourly surface downwelling solar radiation divided by 200 W/m², plus low-cloud fraction. These are simple proxies for heating and local cloud conditions; neither directly measures lift, atmospheric stability or the thermal ceiling. Two additive effects are tested first, without interactions or new splines. No combined history-plus-soaring candidate is fitted in this first comparison.

Rebuild these features over the complete cached hourly timeline, including historical hours whose design files previously omitted radiation/cloud/history. Use precisely the original accumulation convention: the value timestamped t+1 supplies precipitation and solar energy for \[t,t+1); radiation is divided by 3,600 to obtain W/m². Low cloud is the average of the instantaneous endpoints. Require complete predictor coverage and agreement with all saved current-rain values; preserve every observation and mask. Snapshot source SHA-256 hashes. Read existing ERA5 Parquet data only; import no forecast-repository code.

Both candidates use the unchanged training-only smoothing rule: strengths 0.1, 1 and 10, three calendar-week folds, minimum daily MAE with deterministic first-minimum ties. Linear weather and annual offset ridge strengths remain one; new weather coefficients also receive ridge strength one in the stated units. Replay the saved reference for each paired candidate. Save all inner coefficients, out-of-fold predictions, raw solver flags and independent derivative checks. Both maximum gradient and directional derivative error must be below 0.001; no numerical retries or substituted predictions. Review accuracy only after the whole batch finishes.

Use all forty frozen contexts: ten historical seven-day-gap tasks and thirty recent whole-day tasks, all with historical training and the ten core large-bird species. Preserve the existing assessment and gates: historical dry, early dry and reconstructed-total error no worse than the original model; historical wet and recent dry/wet MAE no more than 2% worse than the retained weather reference; median final fit no more than five seconds or five times reference. Report species/year/era, joint year/week bootstrap differences, completeness and runtime. Reused development masks remain exploratory; no production replacement follows automatically. Recent original-model fits use recent training only, whereas both weather candidates use historical training, so that recent comparison is contextual rather than an isolated weather ablation.

Additionally split hidden dry intervals into dry-after-rain versus background dry, using exposure-weighted mean antecedent 24-hour precipitation ≥1 mm. This diagnostic classification is identical for all models, not a species-specific tuning rule. Report missing-count totals and fixed calendar-week sums to expose gains obtained merely by shifting errors between rain and recovery. Weekly sums cover masked recorded intervals only, not complete storm episodes; reconstructed seasonal truth likewise covers eligible recorded intervals rather than unobserved closures. Full storm-plus-recovery closure truth remains unavailable. The mean model still lacks coherent joint predictive uncertainty.

## SoaringMeteo review and later variables

[SoaringMeteo's official guide](https://soaringmeteo.org/soarV2EN.pdf) describes ground heating, thermal height, thermal updraft velocity, boundary-layer winds, cloud layers and atmospheric stability/inversions. Its combined thermal-quality indicator is designed for mountain paragliding, so its wind preferences and composite score should not be transferred directly to migrating birds. The site supplies GFS/WRF forecasts; this inspection does not establish a historical archive suitable for 1993–2025 reconstruction.

The next physically relevant ERA5 additions, if this controlled comparison warrants further work, are boundary-layer height, surface sensible heat flux and wind at migration altitude; vertical temperature/humidity profiles would allow stability, inversions and cloud-base diagnostics. A convective velocity scale requires compatible sensible-heat flux, boundary-layer depth and air temperature, with explicit flux signs and units. CAPE diagnoses potential deep convection and should not be treated as a direct thermal-strength measurement. Field availability and units are documented in [ECMWF’s ERA5 documentation](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=414588701). None of these new fields is downloaded or implemented here. Current radiation and low cloud are available throughout the existing historical cache and therefore make a clean first test. Do not treat a 100-m wind measurement as boundary-layer-average wind or a soaring-height wind profile.

## Completed results and decision

All forty contexts complete for each candidate. All 720 inner fits and 160 final fits pass the unchanged independent numerical gates, including eighty baseline replays. Maximum gradient is 0.000457 and maximum directional derivative error is 4.80e-8; maximum iteration count is 98. Raw BFGS status reports precision loss for 371 of the 880 fits despite passing independent checks; these flags and endpoints remain saved. No retry or fallback is used. The weather snapshot contains 289,272 complete hourly slots across 1993–2025. All 104 repository tests pass.

These are equal-species daily MAEs in birds per masked recorded day; seasonal errors assess reconstructed eligible recorded totals, not unknowable full-season truth. Rain history improves historical dry MAE 1.14%, recent dry 0.39% and recent wet 1.82%; radiation/cloud improves these by 1.31%, 0.34% and 3.53%, respectively, and historical wet by 4.12%. Neither extension produces the major recent dry-day deterioration seen in earlier architectures. All wet/recent protection and speed gates pass; all three historical-original accuracy gates still fail for both candidates. Every core species still has higher reconstructed-season error than the original benchmark.

Paired historical year-bootstrap candidate-minus-reference differences are −0.382 birds [−0.705, −0.051] on dry days for rain history, and −0.436 [−0.799, −0.100] for radiation/cloud. The latter's historical wet difference is −0.855 [−1.988, −0.056]; recent wet difference is −1.057 [−1.597, −0.544] using shared calendar-week blocks. Recent dry intervals overlap zero for both candidates. Rain-history reconstructed-season improvement is −0.266 percentage points [−0.461, −0.083], whereas radiation/cloud is −0.084 [−0.228, +0.065]. Both remain worse than original totals, by +2.30 and +2.48 percentage points respectively. These exploratory intervals do not account for the many earlier development comparisons.

The dry-after-rain diagnostic improves: historical MAE falls from 38.20 to 37.85 with history and 37.53 with radiation/cloud; recent from 52.86 to 51.97 and 52.14. Recent background-dry MAE is effectively unchanged: 46.112 → 46.149 and 46.121. Fixed-week masked-total MAE also improves slightly: historical 83.84 → 83.04 and 82.96; recent 98.91 → 98.39 and 98.74. This is evidence of small gains across more than one aggregation scale, not proof of correct unobserved storm/recovery totals.

Species sensitivity is essential. Honey Buzzard accounts for about 82% of the historical dry aggregate gain from rain history and 62% from radiation/cloud. Removing it only for a diagnostic reduces those dry improvements to 0.24% and 0.59%; it remains in every primary score. Historical dry MAE improves for six species with history and eight with radiation/cloud. Radiation/cloud's wet gains have a different source: Common Buzzard improves 15.5% historically and 11.8% recently, and Red Kite 11.9% and 10.2%. Honey Buzzard wet error worsens 3.8% historically and 2.9% recently with radiation/cloud; rain history worsens its recent wet error 6.9%. Retain these species losses alongside aggregate gains. Fitted radiation/cloud signs vary across species; prediction gains do not establish a universal causal lift response. Rain-band diagnostics also qualify the wet aggregate: radiation/cloud improves 0.1–1 mm/hour mean-rain days but slightly worsens 1–3 mm/hour days (historical MAE 6.73 → 7.00; recent 18.90 → 19.02). There are no historical ≥3 mm/hour mean-rain target days and only ten recent species-day observations in that band, so this test cannot establish performance during sustained heavy-rain closures. These bands use covered-interval mean rain, not maximum hourly intensity.

Both candidates remain small and fast: median final fit is approximately 0.16 seconds and selection plus final fitting approximately 2.2 seconds. Median paired final runtime ratios are 1.18 for history and 0.96 for radiation/cloud; concurrent timing noise precludes claiming a speed gain. The latter does not add a latent daily state or extra seasonal flexibility.

Keep the original production model and retained weather reference. Preserve both two-term extensions as useful experimental evidence, with radiation/cloud the stronger weather-specific next candidate and rain history a modest signal concentrated in Honey Buzzard. Do not declare a universal replacement or automatically combine blocks based on these reused masks. The next physically motivated data extension is boundary-layer height and sensible heat flux, assessed with the same small hourly framework and species safeguards. Station-rain comparison remains a separate later input-quality experiment. Historical partial-day conditioning, joint uncertainty and independent confirmation remain open.

Detailed species/year/era, rain-band, recovery, weekly, seasonal, bootstrap, coefficient and runtime tables are under `logs/era5-features/{rain_history,soaring}/`. `species_sensitivity.csv` records the diagnostic excluding Honey Buzzard and mean species-relative changes without altering primary scores.

It gives wet gains against retained weather but does not improve dry or seasonal reconstruction, and main point estimates trail radiation/cloud. Do not add or stack that pair.
