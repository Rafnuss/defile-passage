# Simple and fast gap filling

> Completed experiment record. Its results are preserved; earlier next-step proposals below are superseded by the [consolidated model decision](MODEL_SPECIFICATION.md). Further experimentation is paused.

## Design constraint

The user requires a simple, fast model. Stop extending the hurdle/shared-Gamma/convolution candidate. Keep its saved results as diagnostics, but do not treat numerical patches or extra latent mechanisms as the default route forward. Model complexity needs a demonstrated improvement on matching validation data and a measured runtime cost.

## First replacement experiment

Fit one species-specific count rate: `log(rate_h) = intercept + s(doy_h) + s(tau_h) + beta_rain * log1p(rain_h)`. The two smooths have seven coefficients each, plus the intercept and rain coefficient: 16 coefficients total. There is no episode term, occurrence hurdle, time-by-season interaction, day random effect or separately modelled positive count. Year effects and additional weather predictors are omitted in this first diagnostic baseline, not established to be unimportant.

`tau` is time relative to civil dawn/dusk. The time knots cover [-1, 2], including observations outside civil daylight; time is not clipped and no hard nighttime zero is imposed. Exposure stays in real hours. The rate is piecewise constant within each canonical clock hour, matching the hourly weather input; arbitrary interval boundaries enter through actual coverage fractions. Subhour rate changes remain outside this approximation.

For any observed interval, `expected_count_i = sum_h(fraction_ih * rate_h)`. Fit that mean directly against the intact observed count. Do not turn survey totals into artificial hourly observations or average their weather before fitting. Prediction means add across temporal scales. This is coherence of the mean, not a validated joint distribution for all possible subdivisions.

Use the penalized Poisson objective as a working mean-fitting criterion, with analytic derivatives and sparse aggregation. This avoids convolution, quadrature and latent-state optimization. It does not establish that bird counts have Poisson dispersion or are independent within a day. Do not use Poisson predictive intervals or occurrence probabilities as calibrated outputs. Week-block refitting is the intended next inference experiment once point predictions are useful, preserving within-day dependence without fitting latent daily states.

The initial smoothing strength is 10 after normalizing each smooth penalty to mean diagonal one. It is a fixed diagnostic choice, not estimated smoothing or a selected optimum. Bases and centering use training years only. Smoothing selection, retained-hour conditioning, annual variation, other weather terms and predictive uncertainty are outstanding. A single linear log-rain coefficient is a small monotonic model whose direction is estimated, not a biological hard-zero rule.

## Staged acceptance

1. Run a diagnostic pilot on Cormorant 2025, Pigeon 2025, Chaffinch 2022 and Redwing 2025. These are known difficult cases, not a new four-species selection panel. Measure runtime and analytic derivative agreement, and expose night, rain and intact-survey predictions.
2. If this baseline is fast and numerically stable, choose smoothing using training-only calendar-week holdouts. Then assess all 16 species across the same annual and gap holdouts. Report daily and hourly error, rainy observed counts, zero-count intervals and intact surveys separately. The pilot cases and repeatedly reused holdouts are exploratory.
3. Add a predictor only when residual diagnostics identify a specific missing effect and held-out gains justify its runtime. Do not restore the full former model by default. If a simple mean model cannot meet the scientific objective, stop and explain which requirement it misses before choosing another architecture.
4. Assess uncertainty and conditioning on retained hours after the mean model is accepted. A small daily adjustment may be justified, but its bias in rainy hidden hours must be checked and it is not part of the baseline.
5. Preserve the existing coverage rules. Historical zeros need verified species protocols; closures remain unobserved. Production replacement requires the full comparison and calibrated uncertainty, not merely faster fitting.

## Bounded predictor development

The baseline is compared with two 20-coefficient specifications. `rain_weather` adds a second log-rain slope above 1 mm/hour, linear wind u/v and previous-24-hour rain. The hinge at `log(2)` lets intermediate and heavy rain differ; its two slopes are learned, not forced negative. Extra weather columns are scaled by their outer-training standard deviations, saved alongside the basis. Neither rain slope is a probability of migration stopping.

`rain_daylight` replaces previous-24-hour rain with an indicator that the canonical hour midpoint is outside civil daylight. It retains the original noncyclic time smooth. The indicator estimates a level difference between daylight and night rather than demanding a single-peaked time profile. A fixed unit quadratic penalty on that one coefficient prevents an infinite estimate when the training night counts are all zero; it is separate from the selected smooth penalty. Night rates remain positive, and the sharp boundary at civil twilight is an explicit approximation. A partly covered hour still uses its canonical midpoint and actual observed duration. This model has no hard-zero night rule and is not a subhour solar-elevation model.

A constrained quadratic/Gaussian time profile was tried and rejected: its constrained optimizers failed numerical checks, and fitting the quadratic with the existing simple optimizer exposed nonconcave Pigeon time profiles in training-week holdouts. Do not add a separate constrained optimizer or force that shape. Saved rejected-trial sources remain under `logs/rejected-constrained-time/` and `logs/rejected-quadratic-time/`.

The four `rain_daylight` pilots pass the numerical checks in 0.19–0.34 seconds including training-only smoothing selection. For the held-out 2025 night hours, Pigeon predicts 0.07 birds against zero observed and Cormorant predicts 53.93 against zero, versus 21 and 2,079 respectively in the baseline selected-smoothing pilot. The remaining Cormorant excess must remain visible. Heavy-rain predictions are still mixed, including 56 Chaffinch birds versus 207 observed and 55 Cormorant birds versus zero. These reused pilot cases guide development; they do not select a production model.

The next assessment runs both the 16-coefficient baseline and 20-coefficient `rain_daylight` model across all 16 species and four held-out years. Compare only common successful contexts and explicitly report their restricted coverage. ## Full-panel rejection and bounded clock-time replacement

The annual baseline/daylight comparison completed 128 context attempts (two candidates × 16 species × four years). The priority batch took 112 seconds and the passerine batch 34 seconds, including per-fit Python startup; the two ran concurrently. Baseline numerical checks pass for 61/64 contexts and `rain_daylight` for 60/64. Failed contexts remain in `status.csv`; comparisons use only common successful contexts. No failed case is silently retried or accepted by relaxing its checks.

Reject the noncyclic-time `rain_daylight` candidate despite its four-case pilot improvement. Its accepted fits predict approximately 941,359 birds in outside-daylight hours containing 25 observed birds and 571,963 in heavy-rain hours containing 1,390 observed birds. Almost all excess comes from Barn Swallow 2025. Its training coordinate reaches only tau −0.022 before dawn, whereas held-out observations extend to around −0.6. The spline log-rate rises steeply in that unsupported range. A fitted night level offset cannot prevent a smooth from continuing to rise there. Numerical convergence is insufficient for scientific acceptance, and a small pilot did not represent the panel's extrapolation failures.

Replace the time spline with three clock-time Fourier harmonics: sine/cosine pairs for frequencies 1, 2 and 3 per 24 hours. This is a bounded periodic log-rate contribution at any clock hour and removes an unbounded time extrapolation mechanism with six coefficients instead of seven. Evaluate phase at each canonical hour's UTC midpoint; changing the fixed time-zone origin rotates sine/cosine coefficients and leaves this model family unchanged, avoiding civil-clock DST discontinuities. Civil dawn/dusk still defines the outside-daylight indicator, so seasonal daylight affects the prediction. This is a clock-time profile plus daylight status, not a periodic transformation of tau with biologically unsupported repeats each daylight duration.

The `cyclic_clock` candidate has 19 coefficients: intercept, two rain slopes, seven seasonal spline coefficients, six clock harmonics, two wind terms and one night coefficient. Harmonic penalties increase as frequency to the fourth power; the same three-strength training-week selection applies. The night coefficient retains its fixed unit penalty. No output cap, hard-zero night assumption, latent day effect or count convolution is introduced. A bounded time contribution does not ensure bounded season/weather extrapolation or good prediction in unsupported combinations. Assess the complete panel and record its failures before accepting this design.

Run the bounded comparison with `--variants cyclic_clock` and output directories `logs/simple-cyclic_clock-panel-{priority,passerines}`. Review it with `uv run python scripts/research/review_simple_panel.py --candidate cyclic_clock`. The review now writes a candidate-specific folder and includes `matched_special_scores.csv`, comparing night, heavy-rain and intact-survey totals on exactly matching successful observations. The earlier unpaired special totals describe each candidate's own available contexts and must not be used as paired model improvement estimates.

## First matched full-day result and numerical resolution

The 22-coefficient year/weather model initially passed 45/48 contexts with BFGS. Its common-context comparison supported a wet-day gain but omitted Pigeon. To assess the entire panel, replace BFGS with a single standard `trust-exact` Newton solver using the analytic Hessian of the same penalized interval-mean objective. This changes neither the predictors nor their penalty or fitted count law. The Hessian is evaluated directly from the aggregated rate derivative; there are no optimizer retries, latent integrations or convolution routines.

The derivative audit now uses Richardson-extrapolated central differences at steps 0.001 and 0.0005 to remove the leading truncation error in large-count species. The acceptance threshold stays 0.001 for both gradient and derivative discrepancy. Independent checks cover the known constant-rate solution with arbitrary exposures, intact mixed-interval derivatives away from the optimum, and multi-million-bird totals. `simple_rate_model.py` contains the two reused numerical routines; `fit_simple_rate.py` remains the linear analysis workflow. Run `uv run python scripts/research/check_simple_rate.py` for those checks.

All 48 year/weather contexts then pass: maximum gradient 0.0000417, maximum derivative discrepancy 0.000000226. Median context time, including smoothing selection, is 0.188 seconds, maximum 0.377 seconds. `logs/simple-gaps-newton-review/` contains the complete 16-species comparison against the original benchmark. Wet-day equal-species MAE is 88.24 versus 135.02 (14/16 species improve), with paired difference −46.77 and week-bootstrap interval −117.75 to −1.86. Dry-day MAE is 153.01 versus 127.57 (10/16 improve), difference +25.43, interval −7.02 to +62.48. The 2,265 wet and 5,806 dry rows are species-days, not unique calendar dates. Heavy-rain hourly totals are 1,269 predicted versus 1,390 observed. On wet observed zero-count days, predicted totals fall from benchmark 54,681 to 12,407, but dry zero-day excess increases from 19,204 to 25,545. The model is promising for wet gaps, not a universal replacement.

Pigeon dry-day MAE is 1.38 times the benchmark; wet-day MAE is 0.45 times. In fold 0 retained training data, observed Pigeon passage centroids range from day 288.8 to 297.7 between years, while one shared season curve misses timing by up to 4.7 days. This motivates one prespecified additional candidate, `year_season_weather`, with six annual linear/quadratic season contrasts. Each annual contrast is multiplied by a training-standardized day-of-year coordinate or its square, with the same fixed unit penalty as other linear deviations. The shared seasonal spline remains. This permits year-specific timing/width variation with 28 coefficients, without an episode smooth or latent daily state. Training-only smoothing selection and the original whole-day masks remain unchanged. Record this as exploratory development on reused masks, not independent confirmation.

The new annual interactions are intended for gaps within observed years. A wholly unobserved year has neither its abundance nor its season contrasts estimated and needs a separately assessed prediction rule. Basis centering, weather scales, season-coordinate scales, named coefficient tables and held-out predictions are saved for reproducibility. The sharp civil-night indicator and unvalidated night/closure support remain limitations; daylight-only benchmark success cannot settle them.

All 48 contexts of the 28-coefficient annual-season candidate pass numerical checks at a median 0.214 seconds including smoothing selection. Its full-day MAE is 134.75 dry and 93.57 wet versus original benchmark 127.57 and 135.02. The paired differences are +7.17 (95% week-bootstrap interval −19.01 to +32.92) and −41.45 (−116.56 to +7.07). Pigeon dry MAE improves to 1.11 times the benchmark, but the model worsens other taxa, including Honey Buzzard. Do not select it universally from its aggregate dry result.

`select_simple_gaps.py` implements a common training-only choice between the 22- and 28-coefficient models: use the lower minimum retained-data calendar-week CV daily MAE, preferring the smaller model on exact ties. It reads validation predictions only after selecting the specification. This uses saved fits rather than repeating optimization, and writes the chosen architecture, both training scores, coefficients and predictions. It selects the smaller model in 32/48 contexts and the larger in 16/48. Its full-panel dry MAE is 154.50 and wet MAE 88.98; it therefore does not resolve the dry-day deficit. Keep this selection experiment as a rejected general replacement, not a reason to choose Pigeon's larger model using its held-out outcomes.

The next bounded experiment changes the working mean criterion, not the predictor structure. With fixed variance power 1.5, the criterion is half the Tweedie unit deviance: `2 * (sqrt(mean) - sqrt(count))^2 / sqrt(mean)`. Its mean score is `(mean - count) / mean^1.5`; analytic derivatives/Hessian are used by the same solver. This reduces the relative influence of high-count flock observations while retaining one positive log-rate and exactly summed interval means. The power is fixed, not tuned on validation or estimated as a new parameter. The smoothing grid is 0.1, 1, 10 for this lower-curvature criterion, chosen by the same inner weekly daily-MAE rule; the fixed unit linear penalty stays unchanged. This is a mean/quasi-deviance fit, not a fitted continuous Tweedie count distribution or a predictive uncertainty model. Dispersion, dependent counts and cross-scale variance remain unvalidated. The variance-power interpretation follows the [mgcv Tweedie documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/Tweedie.html).

Use the 22-coefficient `year_weather` specification with `run_simple_panel.py --variance-power 1.5`, saving to `logs/simple-gaps-tweedie-{priority,passerines}`. Review via `review_simple_gaps.py --prefix simple-gaps-tweedie --out logs/simple-gaps-tweedie-review`. This remains exploratory assessment on reused masks. Do not infer a reliable production replacement from one average score or tune a rain-based switching rule on these held-out truths.
