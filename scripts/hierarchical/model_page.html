A hierarchical model for the hours nobody counted

How model D estimates the birds that passed while no one was counting: a negative binomial model of each day's passage, with daily weather and a day-to-day AR(1) process, read through the time-of-day profile of the hours that were counted. It is production's trend GAM with two changes. Each step below is drawn from the real fits.

1The problem

The count records migrating birds hour by hour, but not every hour of every day of the season. Whole days are missed (rain, a poor forecast, no observer), and counted days start late and end early. To give a season total, a daily series or a passage date, we have to estimate the birds that passed in the uncounted hours, with their uncertainty.

Two facts shape the model. Gaps are not random: missing days follow rain and dark skies, and observers leave once passage dies down. A model that fills a gap with the typical passage for that date therefore over-fills it. Passage is lumpy at every scale: some days carry ten times as many birds as their neighbours, and within a day birds come in flocks. The filling has to keep both kinds of variation.

So the model works at two levels. The upper level models each day's passage: its level from season, year, weather and a day-to-day process. The lower level spreads that day over its hours with the time-of-day profile, and the counted hours are read through it.

2Data

Species. The research's ten core species: Black Kite, Common Buzzard, Common Kestrel, Eurasian Sparrowhawk, European Honey Buzzard, Red Kite, Western Marsh Harrier, White Stork, Great Cormorant and Grey Heron. Each species has its own model; nothing is shared between species.

Counts and effort, 1993–2025. These are the research's frozen inputs. For each species, an observation is an interval of counted time with its count of birds flying in the main direction, split into UTC hours.

3The time-of-day profile and coverage

Counted hours tell us a known share of the day's passage only once we know how passage is spread over the day. The profile \(\pi(d,s)\) is production's estimator, applied to the retained observations.

Samples
We use every retained observation timed to one hour from 2014–2025. An hour counted for at least half its length, on a day with at least two such hours and some birds, gives the ratio of its rate to the day's rate over those hours. Each ratio is weighted by the day's birds to the power 0.5.
\[ r_{h} = \frac{Y_h / e_h}{\sum_{h'} Y_{h'} / \sum_{h'} e_{h'}}, \qquad \text{weight } \big(\textstyle\sum_{h'} Y_{h'}\big)^{1/2} \]
Surface
The ratios are smoothed over the day of the season (1 July–2 December) and over \(\tau_s\), the share of the day's light already elapsed (0 at civil dawn, 1 at civil dusk), with 16 splines in \(\tau\). Dawn and dusk keep their place as the days shorten.
Profile
The surface is read at each five-minute solar slot, multiplied by the slot's daylight fraction \(\delta_s\), and normalised to sum to one each day.
\[ \pi(d,s) = \frac{\hat r(d,\tau_s)\,\delta_s}{\sum_{s'} \hat r(d,\tau_{s'})\,\delta_{s'}} \]

The coverage of an observation is the profile mass of the time it counted, where \(m_{o,s}\) is the fraction of slot \(s\) counted by observation \(o\). A day's coverage is the sum over its retained observations:

\[ c_{yd} = \sum_{o \in (y,d)} \sum_{s} m_{o,s}\,\pi(d,s) \;\in [0, 1]. \]

A day with \(c < 0.1\) tells us nothing about its rate, because a few minutes can hold one flock. It is not fitted, and its birds are added to the day's total as counted (extra).

4The daily model

For each species, the birds counted on day \(d\) of year \(y\) are negative binomial around the day's expected passage times its coverage:

\[ Y_{yd} \sim \operatorname{NegBin}\!\big(\text{mean } c_{yd}\,\Lambda_{yd},\ \text{shape } \theta\big), \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y_{yd}) = c\Lambda + \frac{(c\Lambda)^2}{\theta} \label{eq:nb} \]
\[ \log \Lambda_{yd} = \underbrace{a}_{\text{intercept}} + \underbrace{f_{\text{trend}}(y)}_{\text{12 splines}} + \underbrace{\alpha_y}_{\text{year}} + \underbrace{f_{\text{season}}(d)}_{\text{20 splines}} + \underbrace{f_{\text{shift}}(y,d)}_{6\times 8\ \text{tensor}} + \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{6} \beta_j\, w_{j,yd}}_{\text{daily weather}} + \underbrace{\eta_{yd}}_{\text{AR(1)}} \label{eq:lp} \]

The negative binomial is a gamma-distributed daily rate seen through a Poisson count. Writing it this way is what the gap filling uses:

\[ \lambda_{yd} \sim \operatorname{Gamma}\!\big(\theta,\ \theta/\Lambda_{yd}\big), \qquad Y_{yd}\mid\lambda_{yd} \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(c_{yd}\,\lambda_{yd}). \]

Variation from day to day beyond everything in \(\log\Lambda\) goes into \(\theta\). For Black Kite, \(\hat\theta\) rises from under production to under D, because the weather and the daily process explain part of what production left as noise.

Smooth components

These are production's components, unchanged. They are P-splines: cubic B-splines on equally spaced knots, each with a difference penalty and its own smoothing parameter:

\[ f(x) = \sum_{k} B_k(x)\,b_k, \qquad \text{penalty } \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\, \mathbf b^{\top} \mathbf D_m^{\top} \mathbf D_m\, \mathbf b, \]

where \(\mathbf D_m\) takes \(m\)-th differences of neighbouring coefficients.

componentbasispenalty
\(f_{\text{trend}}(y)\)12 B-splines over the yearssecond differences
\(f_{\text{season}}(d)\)20 B-splines over the window's dayssecond differences
\(f_{\text{shift}}(y,d)\)tensor product, 6 (years) × 8 (days)second differences along each axis (two \(\lambda\))
\(\alpha_y\)one coefficient per yearridge \(\lambda_\alpha \sum_y \alpha_y^2\) (a random effect)
\(\beta_j\)six daily weather termsfixed \(\beta_j \sim N(0,1)\), no smoothing parameter
\(\eta_{yd}\)one value per window day and yearAR(1) precision (section 5)

Each smooth sums to zero over its grid, so each level has one owner. The intercept carries the overall level, \(\alpha_y\) a year's level, and the trend and season the main effects of the shift, which is an interaction only. The constraint is a reparametrisation \(\mathbf b = \mathbf Z \mathbf u\), with \(\mathbf Z\) (\(k\times(k-1)\)) spanning the null space of \(\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf B\):

\[ \sum_{d} f_{\text{season}}(d) = 0, \qquad \sum_y f_{\text{trend}}(y) = 0, \qquad \sum_d \eta_{yd} = 0 \ \text{ for every year } y. \]

All coefficients also carry a tiny ridge (\(10^{-4}\)).

Daily weather

There are six terms per day. Each is a profile-weighted mean over the whole solar day, so it exists for days with no count and gives most weight to the hours that carry passage:

\[ w_{j,yd} = \sum_s \pi(d,s)\; g_j\big(x_{y,d,h(s)}\big), \]
\[ g_j \in \Big\{ \log(1+R),\ \max\!\big(\log(1+R) - \log 2,\ 0\big),\ \text{radiation},\ \text{low cloud},\ u,\ v \Big\}, \]

where \(x_{y,d,h(s)}\) is the weather of the UTC hour holding slot \(s\), and \(R\) its rain in mm. The second rain term is a hinge: a second slope for hours with more than 1 mm of rain. Each term is standardised on the days with a count (mean 0, sd 1), so each \(\beta_j\) is the change in \(\log\Lambda\) per sd of weather.

5The daily process

\(\eta\) replaces production's episode block, which had 25 B-splines a year with first-difference and ridge penalties, about one knot every five days. D has one value per window day and year (about 124 a year), with a stationary AR(1) prior:

\[ \eta_{y,d} = \rho\,\eta_{y,d-1} + \varepsilon_{y,d}, \qquad \varepsilon_{y,d} \sim N\!\big(0,\ \sigma^2(1-\rho^2)\big), \qquad \rho = e^{-1/\tau}, \]
\[ \operatorname{corr}(\eta_{y,d},\ \eta_{y,d+\ell}) = \rho^{\ell} = e^{-\ell/\tau}, \qquad \operatorname{sd}(\eta) = \sigma . \]

In matrix form, a year's \(k\) values have the unit-variance AR(1) precision \(\mathbf R(\rho)\), which is tridiagonal, scaled by \(\lambda_\eta = 1/\sigma^2\), and centred within the year:

\[ \boldsymbol\eta_y = \mathbf Z\mathbf u_y, \qquad \mathbf u_y \sim N\!\Big(\mathbf 0,\ \big(\lambda_\eta\,\mathbf Z^{\top}\mathbf R(\rho)\,\mathbf Z\big)^{-1}\Big), \qquad \mathbf R(\rho) = \frac{1}{1-\rho^2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\rho & & & \\ -\rho & 1+\rho^2 & -\rho & & \\ & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \\ & & -\rho & 1+\rho^2 & -\rho \\ & & & -\rho & 1 \end{pmatrix} \]

Years are independent: the process restarts each season. It lets a hidden day borrow from its neighbours, so a gap in a run of good days is filled high and a gap in a lull is filled low. The correlation length \(\tau\) sets how far that borrowing reaches.

6Fitting

Given the hyperparameters, all coefficients \(\mathbf b\) are Gaussian a priori. There are about 100 dense coefficients, plus 33 year blocks of about 123 daily values. The hyperparameters are the smoothing parameters \(\boldsymbol\lambda = (\lambda_{\text{season}}, \lambda_{\text{trend}}, \lambda_{\text{shift},y}, \lambda_{\text{shift},d}, \lambda_\alpha, \lambda_\eta)\), the shape \(\theta\) and the correlation length \(\tau\):

\[ \mathbf b \sim N\!\big(\mathbf 0,\ \mathbf S_\lambda^{-1}\big), \qquad \mathbf S_\lambda = \sum_k \lambda_k \mathbf S_k + \mathbf I_{\text{weather}} + 10^{-4}\,\mathbf I . \]
Posterior mode
Penalised IRLS (Fisher scoring with step halving) for the negative binomial with log link and offset \(\log c\):
\[ \hat{\mathbf b} = \arg\max_{\mathbf b}\ \sum_{yd} \log \operatorname{NB}\!\big(Y_{yd};\ c_{yd} e^{\mathbf x_{yd}^{\top}\mathbf b},\ \theta\big) - \tfrac12\, \mathbf b^{\top}\mathbf S_\lambda \mathbf b . \]
The penalised information \(\mathbf H = \mathbf X^{\top}\mathbf W\mathbf X + \mathbf S_\lambda\) is solved by blocks: the dense columns, plus one block per year for the daily process. A fit then costs \(O(\text{years}\cdot k^3)\) rather than \(O((\text{years}\cdot k)^3)\).
Smoothing parameters
Extended Fellner–Schall updates (Wood and Fasiolo 2017) find a fixed point of the Laplace marginal likelihood. Each \(\lambda_k\) is scaled by the degrees of freedom its prior allows minus those the data use, divided by the wiggliness it is asked to explain:
\[ \lambda_k \leftarrow \lambda_k\; \frac{\operatorname{tr}\!\big(\mathbf S_\lambda^{-1}\mathbf S_k\big) - \operatorname{tr}\!\big(\mathbf H^{-1}\mathbf S_k\big)}{\hat{\mathbf b}^{\top}\mathbf S_k\hat{\mathbf b}} . \]
The daily process's \(\lambda_\eta = 1/\sigma^2\) is one of them.
Shape θ
θ starts at its maximum likelihood given the fitted means. It is then alternated with the smoothing parameters as the maximiser of the Laplace marginal likelihood, for up to five rounds:
\[ \log p(\mathbf Y\mid\boldsymbol\lambda,\theta,\tau) \approx \ell(\hat{\mathbf b}) - \tfrac12 \hat{\mathbf b}^{\top}\mathbf S_\lambda\hat{\mathbf b} + \tfrac12\log\lvert\mathbf S_\lambda\rvert_+ - \tfrac12\log\lvert\mathbf H\rvert . \]
Correlation length τ
The whole fit is run for τ = 2, 4, 7, 14, 21 and 30 days, and the one with the highest Laplace marginal likelihood is kept. No hyperparameter is chosen on held-out counts.
\[ \hat\tau = \arg\max_{\tau \in \{2,4,7,14,21,30\}} \log p\big(\mathbf Y\mid\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}_\tau,\hat\theta_\tau,\tau\big) \]
Posterior
\(\mathbf b \sim N(\hat{\mathbf b}, \mathbf H^{-1})\). Where a species is counted but never seen, the information is evaluated at no less than 0.1 birds a day (production's MIN_RATE), so the posterior does not become flat there.

Within-day dispersion κ

This is production's hour_dispersion. On days timed to the hour, given the day's birds, the birds of its counted four-hour blocks \(b\) are Dirichlet-multinomial, with weights \(\kappa\) times the share of the profile counted in each block:

\[ (Y_{b})_b \mid \textstyle\sum_b Y_b \sim \operatorname{DirMult}\big(\textstyle\sum_b Y_b;\ \kappa\, c_b\big), \qquad \hat\kappa \text{ by maximum likelihood.} \]

A small κ means birds bunch into a few hours. For Black Kite, \(\hat\kappa\) = per full day of coverage. A D fit takes about 1–2 minutes (six τ, a few cores); a production fit takes 1.5 s.

8Filling the gaps

Each posterior draw (1000 in production, 500 in the assessment) produces a complete season:

coefficients
\[ \mathbf b^{*} \sim N(\hat{\mathbf b}, \mathbf H^{-1}) \]
day's expected passage
\[ \mu_{yd} = e^{\mathbf x_{yd}^{\top}\mathbf b^{*}} \]
day's rate, given its count
\[ \lambda_{yd} \sim \Gamma\!\big(\theta + Y_{yd},\ \tfrac{\theta}{\mu_{yd}} + c_{yd}\big) \]
coverage not counted
\[ m_{yd} = c^{\text{tot}}_{yd} - c_{yd} \]
passage, with flocks
\[ P_{yd} \sim \Gamma\!\big(\kappa m_{yd},\ \tfrac{\kappa}{\lambda_{yd}}\big) \]
birds missed
\[ M_{yd} \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(P_{yd}) \]
\[ \text{day total}_{yd} = Y_{yd} + \text{extra}_{yd} + M_{yd}, \qquad \text{season total}_y = \sum_d \text{day total}_{yd}. \]

The gamma–Poisson pair is conjugate. The rate given the count is a weighted compromise between the model and the day's own counted birds:

\[ \mathbb E[\lambda_{yd}\mid Y_{yd}] = \frac{\theta + Y_{yd}}{\theta/\mu_{yd} + c_{yd}} = w\,\frac{Y_{yd}}{c_{yd}} + (1-w)\,\mu_{yd}, \qquad w = \frac{c_{yd}\,\mu_{yd}}{c_{yd}\,\mu_{yd} + \theta}. \]

So a partly counted day is filled mostly from its own birds once \(c\mu \gg \theta\), and a day with nothing counted (\(c=0\)) draws its rate from the model alone. The flock step widens the fill without moving its mean, because \(\mathbb E[P] = \lambda m\). In production \(c^{\text{tot}}=1\), the whole day; in the assessment it is the coverage of the hidden observations, whose counts are the truth. The mean and quantiles of a season total come from its draws.

9Why the gaps can be filled this way

Filling from a model fitted to the counted hours is unbiased if the gaps depend only on what the model contains: missing at random given the covariates. The missingness audit tested this.

  • Missing days depend on rain (odds ×1.9 per sd), radiation (×0.44 per sd), era, month and weekday. Rain and radiation are now in the model. Once they are, the passage of the neighbouring days no longer predicts a missing day.
  • Early ends follow quiet hours: the hazard of ending falls by 0.78 per sd of the last three hours' passage. The day's counted birds enter the fill through \(\lambda\mid Y\), and with production's profile the hidden tails of early ends are filled without bias (−0.5% to −1.9% of their birds).
  • What the data cannot show: a day that was never counted has no truth. Wet days of 1993–2013 that were counted for six hours or more were mostly days when birds flew, and the uncounted ones probably had fewer. This is why the rain effect is shared across eras (section 12).

10Assessment

Realistic masks hide counted days and hours the way real gaps happen, so their truth is known. There are five draws.

  • Day masks, 1993–2025: a logistic model of a real missing day (era, month, weekend, daylight rain and radiation, whether the day before is missing), fitted on every season day and applied to days counted for six hours or more. Its intercepts are shifted so that each era hides 10% of them:
    \[ \operatorname{logit} P(\text{hide}_{yd}) = \hat\gamma_0 + \delta_{\text{era}} + \mathbf z_{yd}^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\gamma}, \qquad \delta_{\text{era}} : \ \text{mean } P = 0.10 \text{ within the era.} \]
  • Early-end masks, 2014–2025: a discrete-time hazard of a real early end (era, month, time of day, rain in the hour and the three before, the passage of the last three hours), fitted on the afternoon hours of full days. It is shifted so that 30% of those days end early.
  • Days of the lockbox are never hidden.

Scores. The ten species are weighted equally, and scores are averaged over draws. For species \(i\), year \(y\), and the set \(\mathcal H_{iy}\) of hidden day-parts:

\[ \text{season error} = \frac{1}{10}\sum_i \operatorname{mean}_y \frac{\big|\sum_{d\in\mathcal H_{iy}} (\hat M_{iyd} - M_{iyd})\big|}{\sum_d Y^{\text{all}}_{iyd}}, \qquad \text{daily MAE} = \frac{1}{10}\sum_i \operatorname{mean}_{(y,d)\in\mathcal H_i} \big|\hat M_{iyd} - M_{iyd}\big| . \]

We also score wet and dry days, day and early-end masks, eras, and how well the draws' tails are calibrated. Each step is compared with the step before and with production by a paired bootstrap over years, resampling all species and draws together. Keep rule: a step is kept if it improves one of the two primary scores and the other's interval includes zero or is better.

Lockbox: the research's historical calendar phases 1–4 and 8–11, two four-day masks in every 21 days, 1993–2021. They were set aside before any fit and scored once, for the final model and production:

\[ \text{phase}(y,d) = (d - 3 + 4(y - 1993)) \bmod 21 \in \{1,2,3,4,8,9,10,11\}. \]

13Limitations and open questions

  • Real gaps with no counting at all (storm closures, uncounted wet days) have no truth. The masks can only hide counted days, which are less extreme: 27% of masked days had over 5 mm of rain, against 65% of real missing days.
  • The weather effect differs by era on the masks: D fills 1993–2013 wet days 31% low and 2014–2025 wet days 21% high. Whether that is a real change or selection is not settled.
  • Only ten species; passerines and pigeons were not part of this line.
  • Adopting D in production would change the build time (1–2 minutes a taxon) and the meaning of the exported episodes, since the daily process replaces the episode curves. Both repositories would change together.
